# Case Study – The Limits of Transaction Evidence

The banking case exposes a weakness that account-level concurrency tests could not establish. Per-account locks protected balance updates, but `RecordingAccount` saved transaction evidence only after each operation. When a record write failed following a £25 deposit, the balance changed without a corresponding entry. The caller also received an exception despite the completed transaction, raising concerns about the safety of retries.

This finding led to further investigation of how incomplete records affect verification. Visitor could identify discrepancies between recorded movements and account balances, but two unrecorded transactions of equal and opposite value escaped reconciliation. The capstone's additional record-count check detected those omissions. However, replacing both £25 records with £10 entries preserved the count and net movement, allowing mocked model review to proceed despite inaccurate evidence.

These findings suggest that successive verification checks can improve detection without resolving weaknesses in the records they depend on. Coordinated, durable balance-and-record updates and independent transaction-level verification would provide stronger assurance, although their implementation, recovery behaviour and performance remain untested.
